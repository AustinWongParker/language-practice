## 3 · Textbook (5 min): Verbs like gustar
Read the rule out loud, do the exercise, then check the answers. All units: [[Textbook Units]].

These verbs work backwards from English: the **thing** is the subject, and the **person** is the indirect object (**me, te, le, nos, les**).

| Verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| gustar | to like (be pleasing) | **Me gusta** el café. **Me gustan** los perros. |
| encantar | to love (things) | **Nos encanta** viajar. |
| interesar | to interest | ¿**Te interesa** la historia? |
| importar | to matter | No **me importa**. |
| molestar | to bother | **Me molesta** el ruido. |
| doler | to hurt | **Me duele** la cabeza. **Me duelen** los pies. |
| faltar | to be missing | **Nos faltan** dos sillas. |
| parecer | to seem | **Me parece** bien. |
| quedar | to have left / to fit | **Me quedan** cinco dólares. Esta camisa **te queda** bien. |

- Singular thing or verb → **-a**; plural things → **-an**.
- Add **a + person** for clarity or emphasis: **A Ana le gusta. A mí me encanta.**
- Agreeing: **A mí también.** (me too) · **A mí tampoco.** (me neither) · **A mí sí / A mí no.**

**Exercise:** Say it in Spanish.

1. I love dogs. → 
2. My feet hurt. → 
3. Does it bother you? → 
4. Ana likes to read. → 
5. We have two days left. → 
6. "I don't like it." "Me neither." →
