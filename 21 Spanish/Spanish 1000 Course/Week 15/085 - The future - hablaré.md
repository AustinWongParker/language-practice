## 2 · Learn (8 min)
Add these endings to the **whole infinitive**, for -ar, -er and -ir verbs alike:

| | hablar |
|---|---|
| yo | hablar**é** |
| tú | hablar**ás** |
| él / ella / usted | hablar**á** |
| nosotros | hablar**emos** |
| ellos / ustedes | hablar**án** |

- Irregular stems, same endings: **tendr-** (tener), **podr-** (poder), **saldr-** (salir), **vendr-** (venir), **pondr-** (poner), **har-** (hacer), **dir-** (decir), **sabr-** (saber), **querr-** (querer), **habr-** (hay → habrá).
- In conversation **ir a + infinitive** is more common for plans (**voy a viajar**). The future sounds a bit more certain or formal, and is also used to guess: **¿Dónde estará?** = Where can it be?
- Time words: **pronto, mañana, el próximo año, la semana que viene, en el futuro**.

## 4 · Talk with your partner (10 min)
See the [[Conversation Partner Guide]] for how to run this.

**Warm-up drill (3 min):** your partner reads the English; you say it in Spanish. They check the answer key.

1. I will call you tomorrow.
2. We will travel next year.
3. Will you have time?
4. They will come soon.
5. What will you do in the future?

> [!check]- Answer key (for your partner)
> 1. Te llamaré mañana.
> 2. Viajaremos el próximo año.
> 3. ¿Tendrás tiempo?
> 4. Vendrán pronto.
> 5. ¿Qué harás en el futuro?

**Conversation (7 min):** your partner asks, you answer in full sentences. Try to use at least three of today's words.

- **¿Qué harás mañana?** _(What will you do tomorrow?)_
- **¿Dónde vivirás en cinco años?** _(Where will you live in five years?)_
- Then switch: you ask your partner the same questions.

Afterwards, add the best correction you got to [[Spanish Mistakes Log]].
