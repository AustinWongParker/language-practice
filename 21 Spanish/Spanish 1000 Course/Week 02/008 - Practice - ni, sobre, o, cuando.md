## 3 · Textbook (5 min): Tú vs usted: formal and informal "you"
Read the rule out loud, do the exercise, then check the answers. All units: [[Textbook Units]].

Spanish has two ways to say "you" to one person, and two for a group:

| | One person | More than one |
|---|---|---|
| Informal | **tú** hablas | **vosotros** habláis (Spain only) |
| Formal | **usted** habla | **ustedes** hablan |

- **Tú**: friends, family, kids, people your age, anyone who uses it with you.
- **Usted**: strangers older than you, customers, officials, the elderly, formal situations. When in doubt, start with usted; people will say **"tutéame"** (call me tú) if they want.
- **Usted / ustedes** use the **él / ellos** verb forms: **¿Cómo está usted?** · **¿Ustedes hablan inglés?**
- In Latin America **ustedes** is the only plural "you", friendly or formal. In Spain friends are **vosotros**.
- Possessives follow along: tú → **tu** casa; usted → **su** casa.
- In writing: **Querido/a** (dear, informal) vs **Estimado/a** (dear, formal); sign off **Un abrazo** (informal) or **Atentamente** (formal).
